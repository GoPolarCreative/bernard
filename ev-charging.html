Buy The Charger Second, Not First

Most EV charger quotes go wrong in the same place. Someone buys a 22kW charger, then finds out the house is on a single phase supply and an old board with no spare ways. We look at the supply first.

What your supply can carry

Single phase or three phase, the size of your main switch and what the rest of the house already draws. This is the number that decides which chargers are even on the table.

Whether the board has room

A charger needs its own circuit and its own RCD protection. Older boards often have no spare ways, which makes a switchboard upgrade part of the job.

How far the cable has to run

Metres of cable, and whether the route is a clean run through the roof space or a trench across a driveway. This is usually the biggest single line on the quote.

Which Charger Actually Suits You

Charge speeds below are rough figures for a typical electric car. Your own car has a maximum AC charge rate built into it, and a 22kW charger will not go any faster than the car allows.

11kW three phaseHomes with three phase, two car householdsThree phase supply and a dedicated circuitAround 60 km
22kW three phaseWorkplaces, fleet parking, homes with heavy daily distanceThree phase supply, a larger main switch and often a board upgradeAround 120 km, if your car accepts it

Swipe the table sideways

Most electric cars sold in Australia accept between 7kW and 11kW on AC. Very few accept the full 22kW, so a bigger charger is often money spent on capacity the car cannot use. We will tell you which one fits your car rather than selling you the biggest box.

Load Management Beats An Upgrade

If your supply is tight, the answer is not always a bigger service. A charger with load management watches what the rest of the house is drawing and turns itself down when the oven and the ducted heating are both running. It is usually cheaper than upgrading the mains, and it is one of the first things we look at.

How The Install Runs

Five Steps, No Guesswork

Step 01

Supply And Load Check

We look at the switchboard, the main switch, the meter and what the house already draws, then work out the maximum demand with the charger added.

Step 02

Charger And Position Advice

Which charger suits your car and your supply, and where it should go so the cable reaches the car without crossing a walkway.

Step 03

Paperwork Where It Is Needed

Some installs need the electricity distributor notified or the connection approved before the charger goes on. We handle that rather than leaving it to you.

Step 04

Install And Circuit

Dedicated circuit run from the board, RCD protection, isolator and the charger mounted and terminated. Cable routed neatly, not draped.

Step 05

Test, Commission And Certify

Full electrical test, the charger commissioned and set to the right current, app or load settings configured, and a Certificate of Electrical Safety issued.

Pricing

What Moves The Number

We quote every EV install on site rather than off a price list, because these six things change the figure more than the charger does.

01

Distance from the switchboard to the charger

The single biggest variable. A garage next to the board is a different job to a carport at the far end of the block.

02

The cable route

A clean run through the roof space is straightforward. Trenching under a driveway or chasing through a rendered wall is not.

03

Whether the board has a spare way

If it does not, a switchboard upgrade becomes part of the job. We tell you that upfront, not halfway through.

04

Single phase or three phase

Three phase installs cost more in cable and hardware, and are only worth it if your car and your supply can both use it.

05

Main switch and supply capacity

If the maximum demand with the charger added exceeds your supply, the answer is either load management or a supply upgrade.

06

Who supplies the charger

You can buy your own and we will install it, or we can supply it. Either way the labour and materials are itemised separately so you can see what you are paying for.
