Warning Signs

Six Reasons Boards Get Replaced

If more than one of these sounds like your place, the board is doing you no favours. None of them are urgent on their own. Together they usually mean the board is older than the wiring behind it.

Ceramic fuses and fuse wire

If you are still rewiring a fuse with wire off a card, the board predates safety switches entirely.

Breakers that trip for no reason

Nuisance tripping is usually a board or a circuit telling you something, not a fault to be reset and ignored.

No safety switches, or only one

One RCD covering the whole house means a fault anywhere kills power everywhere, including the fridge and the alarm.

No spare ways left

Every way used means the next air conditioner, EV charger or shed circuit cannot go in without a board change anyway.

Scorch marks, buzzing or heat

Any browning around a fuse holder, a burning smell or a buzz from the board is a call now, not a call later.

Nothing labelled, nothing known

If nobody can tell which switch does what, nobody can isolate safely in an emergency.

Before And After

What Actually Changes

A switchboard upgrade is not cosmetic. It changes what happens in the half second after something goes wrong behind a wall.

Before

Old rewirable fuse board

  • Fuse wire that has to be replaced by hand, often with the wrong gauge
  • No residual current protection, so a person becoming part of the circuit is not detected
  • One fault takes out more of the house than it needs to
  • No spare capacity for new circuits
  • Circuits unlabelled and unknown
  • Enclosure often open, unsealed or heat damaged

After

Compliant modern switchboard

  • Circuit breakers that trip and reset, sized correctly to the cable
  • RCD protection on the circuits the Wiring Rules require it on, tested before handover
  • Faults isolated to the circuit they happen on, so the rest of the house keeps running
  • Spare ways left deliberately for future circuits
  • Every circuit identified, tested and labelled
  • Sealed enclosure, correct main switch and a surge device where you want one

Scope

What Goes Into An Upgrade

01

New enclosure and main switch

Correctly sized and sealed, with room to work in and room to grow.

02

Circuit breakers sized to the cable

Not to the appliance and not to whatever was there before. Every breaker matched to what it is actually protecting.

03

RCD protection

Safety switches fitted to the circuits the Wiring Rules require them on, and tested with an instrument rather than by pressing the button.

04

Main earth and bonding checked

The part nobody sees and the part that decides whether the safety switches can do their job. Checked and brought up to standard as part of the upgrade.

05

Circuit identification and labelling

Every circuit traced, tested and labelled, so the next person at the board knows what they are switching off.

06

Surge protection if you want it

Optional, and worth discussing if you have solar, a home office or a lot of electronics on the same supply.

07

Test results and certification

Full test sheet and a Certificate of Electrical Safety for the prescribed work, handed to you before we leave.

How It Runs

A Board Swap, Start To Finish

Step 01

Look At What Is There

A photo of the open board is often enough to quote from. If not we come out. Either way the quote is free and the price is upfront.

Step 02

Book The Power Off Window

Most single phase upgrades are a same day job with the power off for part of it. We tell you roughly how long before the day, not on it.

Step 03

Change Over And Test

Old board out, new board in, every circuit reconnected, identified and tested with an instrument.

Step 04

Label, Certify And Explain

Board labelled, test results recorded, Certificate of Electrical Safety issued, and two minutes showing you which switch does what.

Pricing

What Changes The Price

Every board is different, which is why we quote them individually rather than off a flat rate. These are the things that move it.

01

How many circuits the house has

A two bedroom unit and a five bedroom house with ducted heating and cooling are not the same job.

02

Single phase or three phase

Three phase boards carry more hardware and take longer to change over.

03

The condition of the existing wiring

If the mains, the earth or the circuits coming into the board are not up to standard, that work is separate and we will show it separately.

04

Whether the enclosure has to move

Sometimes the meter box is the wrong size or the wrong position and the board has to be relocated or extended.

05

Extras you want fitted

Surge protection, spare ways, an EV charger circuit or a separate garage or shed supply added at the same time.
